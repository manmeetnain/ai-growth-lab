# Strategy

## The core problem with "do everything"

Chasing money, recognition, connections, event invites, and startup betas as five separate
efforts fails because each one alone has almost no credibility behind it — a cold DM asking for
beta access, a conference talk submission with no track record, a freelance pitch with no
portfolio. Companies and event organizers filter on proof of work, not intent.

## The flywheel

Everything routes through one artifact:

```
BUILD one real, working, demoable thing (the capstone)
   -> SHOW it (build-in-public content, launch posts, demo videos)
      -> CONNECT it to gatekeepers (startups' beta/design-partner programs,
         event CFPs, webinar co-hosts, freelance leads)
         -> MONETIZE (consulting off the back of it, paid pilots, eventually
            a hosted/paid version)
            -> the money + connections fund and motivate the NEXT iteration
               of the same artifact, compounding audience and network
```

The capstone is not "a portfolio piece" in the resume sense — it's the thing that makes every
cold outreach warm. "I built X, here's the repo/demo, I noticed you're working on Y, here's
where they overlap" is a fundamentally different message than "hi, can I get beta access."

## Why one thing, not many

- Depth beats breadth for credibility. One thing that's genuinely good and has real users/stars/
  usage data outcompetes five half-finished demos.
- Startups running beta/design-partner programs want people who will actually use and stress
  the product — a single serious project signals that; a scattered GitHub profile doesn't.
- Content compounds when it's about one evolving thing (a build-in-public thread with a
  throughline) far better than disconnected one-off posts.
- Time is the actual constraint, not ideas. Optionality feels safe but produces nothing anyone
  outside your head can point to.

## Priority ordering (per your answers)

1. **Money** — freelance/consulting work and, if it gets traction, a paid tier of the capstone.
2. **Startup access** — beta/design-partner programs at AI infra & agent-tooling companies.
3. Recognition/network is not the stated top priority, but it's the *mechanism* that produces
   both 1 and 2 — you don't get consulting leads or beta invites without being visible to the
   people who grant them. So content + events stay in the plan as infrastructure, not vanity.

## What "done" looks like at 90 days

- A working, publicly demoable v1 of the capstone with real usage (not just code — actual
  people/agents running it).
- At least 3 substantive outreach conversations with target companies (TARGETS.md), ideally 1
  beta/design-partner relationship started.
- At least 1 paid engagement (even small — a paid pilot, a consulting day, a bounty) traceable
  to the capstone.
- A content trail (posts/threads/repo README/demo video) that a stranger can follow to understand
  what you built and why, unprompted.

Re-evaluate the capstone choice at day 30 and day 60 checkpoints in ROADMAP.md — kill or pivot
it if usage/interest signal is genuinely absent, but give it a real shot first; the flywheel only
works if you don't restart it every three weeks.
